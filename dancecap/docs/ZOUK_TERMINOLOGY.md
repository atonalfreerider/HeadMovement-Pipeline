# Brazilian Portuguese terminology for the Zouk1 moves

Decided 2026-10-06. Data file: `taxonomy/pt_br_terminology.json` (one row per distinct node name in `Zouk1.json`, 117 rows, with every source URL). This page summarises it.

## Policy

- The canonical name is an attested Brazilian Portuguese term. The user's English name is kept as an alias.
- If no Portuguese term is attested for a move, the user's name stays canonical. A plain Portuguese description may be given, marked *descritivo*; it is a description, not a term.
- Every term comes from fetched sources: Brazilian zouk schools and teachers, lesson video titles and descriptions, the BZDC/BZWC syllabus and rules, the CNDD zouk syllabus, and Brazilian theses (UFPA 2019, UFRGS 2012, UFRJ 2021) and articles (SciELO 2023, FIEP). No name was invented and no literal translation is presented as a term.
- Words found only in other Brazilian dances are not used for zouk (for example *caranguejo* and *cadeirinha* from samba de gafieira, *contragiro* from lambada).
- Display capitals: Title Case with lower-case prepositions and articles, as in *Giro no Eixo* (UFRJ) and *Frente e Trás* (BZDC).

## The Corredor decision

**Lateral becomes Corredor** (the user's decision). Spelling: *Corredor*, no accent, masculine (*o corredor*), plural *corredores*. *Lateral* stays an alias.

- **Meaning.** The follower walks a line back and forth in front of the leader and pivots (*pisou-virou*) at each end, while the leader moves sideways on another line. This matches the user's definition. Source: [Costa, UFPA thesis (2019)](https://bdm.ufpa.br/handle/prefix/5234), which also says the move is *"mais conhecido como corredor, pois é assim que se chama no Rio de Janeiro"* and credits Adílio Porto and Renata Peçanha with it.
- **Official use.** The CNDD (Confederação Nacional de Dança Desportiva) *Syllabus Bronze Dança de Salão Zouk* lists [Figura 06 "Corredor"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ixk8oYY4vjg) and [Figura 09 "Giro com corredor"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vgfcKGTqlbg), published 2022-12-28. It is the only standardisation body found that uses the term.
- **Long-standing usage.** [Philip Miha & Fernanda Teixeira, "Passo do Corredor" (2012)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kUHF1ogfc60); [Paulo Aguiar DVD, "Corredor fechado e aberto (Básico 3)"](http://www.pauloaguiar.com/curso-de-zouk%20vol1.html); [Sarah e Léo, "Aula 02 - Corredor" (2021)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YYamPZBlauE); [LD Escola, "O Corredor" (2022)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KmQTAL3eowM); [Helen Avelar, "LATERAL/CORREDOR" (2021)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tyrFPUnYNUM); [Andrei Udiloff, "o famoso corredor (também conhecido como lateral)"](https://andreiudiloff.com.br/produto/aprenda-a-dancar-zouk-em-apenas-1-final-de-semana/).
- **Still called Lateral by:** the [BZDC educational page](https://www.brazilianzoukcouncil.com/educational) and its syllabus demo ["Zouk Brasileiro: Lateral" (2026-10-02)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WEW4IP71lJo); the BZWC texts from 2019 to [2026](https://www.brazilianzoukworldchampionships.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-Zouk-Worlds-Regras.pdf) (the 2019 content glosses it "Lateral (Corridor)"); [SciELO 2023](https://www.scielo.br/j/rbep/a/rNhzqXVKxSQHzYbJVB66Q6h/?lang=pt); and the [Embrace glossary](https://www.embracezoukonline.com/courses/glossary-fundamentals), which lists "Lateral (Corredor, Infinity)".
- **Recognition: not found online.** No council, congress or syllabus body was found announcing, with a date, that *Corredor* replaces *Lateral*. The only documented zouk naming vote (2006, Minas/BH Zouk Congress) chose the name of the dance. Cite CNDD, or the user's own source, for the recognition.
- **Not adopted.** The alias *Infinity* is left out, because one school uses it for *bate cabelo*. *Lateral From Behind* has no Portuguese name; see the questions below.

## The "cicada" move

**Unresolved: the user needs to say what the move does.**

- **Sacada** ("sa-KA-da") is the closest match by sound. It means a leg displacement (*sacada de perna*) in [samba de gafieira](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lfGdGqmZr2g), Brazilian bolero, forró and tango. No Brazilian zouk syllabus, thesis, glossary or lesson title found uses it. One zouk school abroad does: ["Zouk Lab #8: ... Shooting the Leg (Saccada de Perna)" (2025)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X5nR8zfspbY). If this is the move, label it as a loan from gafieira and tango.
- **Chicote** ("shee-KOH-chee") sounds less like *cicada* but is well attested in zouk. It is a fast whip of the head and hair: [CNDD zouk syllabus, Figura 13 "Chicote"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LOG8JYuy05M); [Helen Avelar](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lShLUTviiA0): *"Chicote é um tipo de movimento de cabeça mais rápido no Zouk"*; [Sérgio Telles, "aula chicote (dama)", started from the corredor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oeEyFmAsCTk). If this is the move, the user's **Pop** node is the likely owner.
- **Sarrada** ("sa-HA-da") is a hip-led zouk move ([Online Zouk School course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dtJ26WoMax0)), a moderate match by sound.
- *Cigarra*, the Portuguese word for cicada, is not a zouk term.

## Questions only the user can settle

1. **Corredor recognition.** Which body or event made *Corredor* the official term, and when? The only official use found online is the CNDD Bronze syllabus (2022). The BZDC still said *Lateral* on 2026-10-02.
2. **Lateral From Behind.** Should it become *Corredor From Behind*, which mixes Portuguese and English, or stay *Lateral From Behind*? No Portuguese name exists, and *corredor por trás* would be a calque.
3. **"Cicada".** Does the move displace the partner's leg (*sacada*), whip the head and hair (*chicote*), or press with the hips (*sarrada*)?
4. **Lunges.** Sources treat *Lunge*, *Preparação para Giro* and *Abertura* as one fundamental. Here Side Lunge is *Preparação para Giro*, Lunge Forward is *Lunge*, and Open Lunge and open Lunge In Front keep English. Which node is the fundamental, and do you prefer the loanword or the native form? Note: the current `zouk_graph.json` shows Side Lunge as *Abertura*, which this research did not choose because *abertura* also means opening the embrace.
5. **Bônus, Boomerang, Reverse Turn.** Many sources treat *Bônus*, *Boomerang* and *Giro Invertido* as one move. Are these three distinct moves? Do you want the dictionary spelling *Bumerangue* or *Boomerang*, which is how BZWC and most Brazilian teachers write it?
6. **Cestinha.** It is assigned provisionally to Hold Behind Crossed Arms. Is it that node, wrap Hug, or the Cross wrap entry?
7. **Little Turn and Viradinha.** *Viradinha* literally means "little turn". Are these two nodes the same move? If so, merge them.
8. **Waves.** Sources use *onda* and *cobrinha* for the same general zouk wave. Are *Onda* (Do The Wave), *Cobrinha* (Snake) and *Ondulação* (Body Roll) acceptable as three labels?
9. **Head movements.** Is Pop the *chicote* (or Luca e Amanda's *explosão*)? Is Behind Back Howl the *toalha* (also called *tornado* or *desengrenado*)? Is Roll the *rolinho* (rotisserie)?
10. **Uncertain matches to confirm.** Crosswalk = *Passo Cruzado* (this may be the corredor's own footwork). Two Hands Armbar = Paulo Aguiar's *Chave de Braço*. Dive = *Mergulho* (sources mean a head descent; your node is a low step). Lead Turn = the leader's own turn (*Giro do Cavalheiro*), not a turn led for the follower. Pirouette = *Giro no Eixo*, which is leader-driven. Bent Spin = *Giro Inclinado*.
11. **Unverified candidate names.** Turn Wrap or Wrap Behind = *Enrosca*? Walk Around = *Contorno na Dama*? Arm Across Shoulders = *Colar* or *Guilhotina*? Spring Load = *Mola*? Step To Side = the side basic (*Básico para o Lado*)?
12. **Icons and duplicates.** Gostosinho is a Tetra, but sources describe it as a basic or embrace. Two Hand howl Down is a Diamond. Are the two Turn nodes the same turn?
